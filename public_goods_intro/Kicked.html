Study Debriefing

Dear participant, thank you for taking part in this study.

This research investigates how people make investment decisions about shared infrastructure and how cooperation develops over repeated interactions. The design is based on a scenario involving the transition to electric freight transport.

In this study, participants took the role of transport companies and decided how much of their budget to invest in a shared electric truck charging infrastructure. Each round represented one year. The total investment made by the group was increased by a fixed factor representing public support for infrastructure development and then distributed equally among all companies, regardless of how much each company invested. This decision task is based on a well-known economic decision paradigm that is often used to study cooperation in groups.

This decision environment captures a common challenge in sustainability transitions: investments in shared infrastructure can benefit everyone in the long term, but individual organizations may also have incentives to prioritize their own short-term resources.

Before and after the interactive task, participants were asked to complete several short questionnaires about expectations, attitudes, and individual characteristics (for example, views about trust and sustainability). These measures help us understand how expectations are formed, and how individual differences relate to decision-making and cooperation in situations like the one presented in this study.

In some cases, interactive sessions may have ended early if a participant did not respond within the required time. This feature is necessary to maintain the real-time interaction of the task.

The results of this research will help us better understand how cooperation emerges in sustainability transitions that require coordinated investments, such as shared charging infrastructure in the transport sector.
