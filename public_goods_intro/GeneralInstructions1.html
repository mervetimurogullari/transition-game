This study is based on a real-life challenge for transport companies.

The sector is undergoing a sustainability transition. To reduce carbon emissions, diesel trucks are gradually being replaced by electric trucks.

From 2026, highway charges for diesel trucks will increase. This policy is intended to support the transition to electric transport.

The challenge: Insufficient charging infrastructure

Electric trucks require sufficient charging infrastructure to work well. Building this network requires substantial investments.

Most companies have basic private chargers, but the public charging network is still too small. As a result, the transition to sustainable freight transport is slower and more difficult.

Collaboration:

Companies can work together to build a shared charging network. All companies can use these charging stations, even if they did not invest in building them.

Each company has an annual budget of €2 million for investment in charging infrastructure. This budget is partly supported by government policy.

Each year, companies decide how much of this budget to:

  • Invest in the shared network
  • Keep for their own company
The dilemma:
If you choose to invest:
  • The shared network grows more quickly.
  • This supports the transition to electric transport.
If you choose not to invest:
  • You keep more money for your own company now.
  • You may still benefit from the shared network if other companies invest.

Keeping the money is tempting because companies that do not invest may have more resources available for their own operations. However, if everyone does this, the network remains limited and the transition becomes more difficult.

Will you invest in the shared charging network, or keep the money for your company?
